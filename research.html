Research interests and projects

My research interests connect theoretical biophysics, physics-based computational chemistry and the emergence of complex behaviour through local interactions.

Molecular and quantum chemistry

My interests include molecular dynamics, quantum chemistry and fragment molecular orbital analysis of open- and closed-shell systems, alongside methods for describing strongly correlated electronic systems. I am particularly interested in how computational methods can clarify the physical basis of molecular behaviour.

Metalloproteases and the extracellular matrix

My biological interests centre on matrix metalloproteases, particularly MMP1 and the membrane-anchored MMP14, together with membrane-protein interactions and lipid bilayer modelling.

Collagen remodelling in osteogenesis imperfecta

This project examines how changes in collagen structure affect its interaction with MMP1. It uses molecular simulation to investigate structural questions motivated by osteogenesis imperfecta, while keeping molecular observations distinct from claims about disease causation.

Complexity and molecular space-time

My interest in complexity theory has led me to fractal geometry, Lindenmayer systems and cellular automata. I explore how these mathematical ideas connect with chemistry and the life sciences, particularly how local interactions generate organised behaviour across a larger system.

Strange Matter Engine

Molecular graph cellular automata pass numerical information between bonded atoms. Retaining the evolving states produces molecular space-time trajectories, which we can study as dynamical systems and use to construct predictive features.
